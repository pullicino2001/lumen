# Leica Q2 / Q3 — Camera Profile

**Type:** Fixed-Lens Mirrorless (Rangefinder-style body)  
**Lens (fixed):** Leica Summilux 28mm f/1.7 ASPH  
**Sensor:** Q2: 47.3MP full-frame CMOS | Q3: 60MP BSI CMOS  
**Production:** Q2: 2019–present | Q3: 2023–present  
**ISO Range:** Q2: 50–50,000 | Q3: 50–100,000  
**Autofocus:** Contrast-detect AF (fast and accurate)  
**Significance:** The most commercially successful Leica of the modern era. Accessible Leica image quality with the convenience of AF and fixed focal length.

---

## Visual Identity

The Q2 and Q3 occupy a unique position: they are the first Leicas that many people ever shoot, because they remove the barriers of rangefinder focusing and lens selection while keeping Leica's core image quality. The fixed 28mm Summilux f/1.7 is a remarkable lens — sharp across the frame even wide open, with controlled vignetting and a rendering style that sits between the classic Leica drawing and a more modern clinical look.

The Q series images have a distinctive quality: sharp and well-saturated but not clinical. The 28mm perspective has a slight environmental quality — you're always capturing subject-in-world rather than subject-isolated. Even at f/1.7, the depth of field at 28mm means more context remains in frame compared to a 50mm at equivalent aperture.

The Q3's 60MP sensor is essentially the same BSI technology as the M11 — the colour science and dynamic range are near-identical. The Q3 also adds a tilting rear screen and improved autofocus, including a hybrid OVF/EVF.

---

## Fixed Lens: Summilux 28mm f/1.7 ASPH

This lens was designed specifically for the Q cameras and is not available in M-mount. It has its own distinct rendering character:

**Sharpness:** Outstanding across the entire frame even at f/1.7. Most lenses this fast are sharp in the centre and fall off at the corners — the Q lens maintains excellent corner performance. This makes it exceptional for architecture and landscapes where edge sharpness matters.

**Bokeh:** At f/1.7, the 28mm focal length doesn't produce the dramatic subject isolation of a 50mm f/1.4 or 85mm f/1.2 — the depth of field is simply too wide at most distances. But the bokeh that is visible (close focus distances, background at distance) is smooth and well-controlled.

**Vignetting:** Visible at f/1.7 — darkening in the corners that Leica applies intentionally as a slight border light falloff. At f/4 and above, vignetting is nearly gone.

**Distortion:** Extremely low for a 28mm lens. The Summilux 28mm is optically corrected and then further corrected in-camera — straight lines remain straight.

**Colour transmission:** Rich and neutral with the slight warmth characteristic of all Leica Summilux optics.

## Colour Science (Q2 / Q3)

The Q cameras use the same Leica colour science as the M system cameras. The Q2's 47MP sensor has a slightly different rendering to the Q3's 60MP BSI, but both share:

**Skin tones:** Warm, smooth, flattering. Leica's colour matrix is particularly well-regarded for portraits — the warm tint in skin is intentional and consistent.

**Blues:** Clean and deep. The Q's wide 28mm captures lots of sky — the sensor renders sky blues with excellent saturation and graduation.

**Reds/oranges:** Rich and saturated without clipping. Architecture, flowers, fabric — the Q's reds are a strong point.

**Shadows:** Both cameras have excellent shadow recovery. The Q3's BSI sensor is especially impressive — equal to the M11 in dynamic range.

---

## Rendering Character

**Contrast:** The Q2 has a slightly higher native contrast rendering than the Q3. Both can be adjusted. The Q2's JPEG processing has a certain "pop" that Q3 users sometimes miss.

**Sharpness:** Both are extremely sharp. The Q3's 60MP fully resolves the 28mm lens's optical quality at every aperture.

**Depth of field character:** The Q's fixed 28mm creates a particular kind of image — wide, environmental, immersive. Even close-focus portraits include surroundings. This is neither a limitation nor a feature — it is the 28mm way of seeing.

**Digital crop modes:** The Q cameras offer digital crop to 35mm, 50mm, and 75mm equivalents while maintaining enough pixels for practical use. The Q3 at 75mm crop still provides ~15MP — enough for most uses.

## Shooting Conditions

**Street photography:** The Q2/Q3's AF, fixed 28mm, and compact body make it ideal for street. Images have the classic street photography perspective — close to subjects, everything in context.

**Architecture:** The wide 28mm and corner-to-corner sharpness make the Q cameras exceptional for architecture. Combined with the Q3's 60MP and minimal distortion, architectural detail is extraordinary.

**Travel:** One camera, one lens, everything. The Q series is designed for this — and the images show it. Environmental quality, sharpness, Leica colour, without the rangefinder learning curve.

**Low light:** The f/1.7 maximum aperture and excellent high ISO give genuine low-light capability. The Q3's ISO 100,000 is usable in emergency conditions.
