# Nikon FM2 / F3 — Camera Profile

**Type:** 35mm Film SLR  
**FM2 Production:** 1982–2001 | F3 Production: 1980–2001  
**Format:** 35mm film (24×36mm frame)  
**Metering:** FM2: centre-weighted TTL | F3: centre-weighted with multiple exposure modes  
**Significance:** The FM2 is the benchmark mechanical 35mm SLR. The F3 is Nikon's definitive professional film camera — used by photojournalists worldwide through the 1980s–1990s.

---

## Visual Identity

Nikon's film cameras represent the photojournalism aesthetic. Where Leica represents the considered, personal, decisive-moment tradition, Nikon represents the relentless, professional documentary tradition — sports, war, politics, news. The FM2 and F3 are associated with some of the most important documentary photography of the late 20th century.

The FM2/F3's rendering is entirely lens + film stock. What these cameras add to the aesthetic is the *SLR perspective* and the character of Nikon's metering approach:

**Metering character:** Nikon's centre-weighted metering reads a slightly wider circle than many cameras, tending to produce well-exposed midtones at the slight cost of shadow and highlight detail at the extremes.

**SLR mirror:** The F3's mechanical SLR mirror produces a slight vibration — especially at 1/30s–1/8s where shutter speeds are marginal. Images at these speeds often have a slight softness that is characteristic of SLR hand-held photography.

**Viewfinder:** The F3's viewfinder shows exactly what the lens captures — unlike the Leica rangefinder's separate optical system. This SLR approach tends toward more documentary, action-oriented composition.

---

## Rendering Character

Nikon's film cameras are associated with Nikon's G and AI-S series lenses. The 50mm f/1.4 AI-S is the quintessential Nikon standard lens — sharp, slightly clinical, with excellent micro-contrast.

**Nikon lens colour:** Slightly cooler than Leica lenses. The Nikkor coating produces a neutral-to-cool colour transmission — not aggressively cool, but without the warmth of Leica's Summilux series.
