# Fujifilm Acros 100 II — Film Stock Profile

**Type:** Black and White Negative  
**ISO:** 100 (Acros 100 II: 2018–present)  
**Format:** 35mm, 120  
**Production:** Original Acros: 2003–2018 | Acros 100 II: 2018–present  
**Significance:** Considered the finest B&W film currently in production. Exceptional tonal range, extremely fine grain, unique reciprocity characteristics.

---

## Visual Identity

Acros 100 is the reference for what black and white photography can be — technically. It is the benchmark against which all other B&W films are measured for grain, sharpness, and tonal range. Unlike Tri-X, which achieves its effects through contrast and grain character, Acros achieves its effects through tonal precision — a rendering of the grey scale that is more complete and accurate than any other commonly available B&W stock.

The result is images with extraordinary shadow detail, smooth tonal transitions, and extremely fine grain that at 35mm approaches the apparent resolution of medium-format film. These images read less like photojournalism and more like fine-art prints — technically refined, tonally rich, with a quality that rewards large-format printing.

---

## Tonal Response

**Shadows:** Deep but not crushed. Acros retains shadow detail in regions that would go completely black on Tri-X. This is perhaps Acros's greatest distinction — shadow areas have genuine tonal information, not just grain in darkness.

**Shadow-midtone transition:** Very smooth. The transition from shadow to lower midtone is gradual — one of the smoothest of any B&W film. This produces a quality of depth in shadow areas that appears three-dimensional.

**Midtones:** Full and rich. Acros's midtone rendering gives skin, fabric, and textured surfaces a quality that reads like analogue depth — not digital smoothness, but organic tonal richness.

**Highlights:** Gradual rolloff. Acros handles overexposed highlights gracefully — the shoulder of the film curve is longer than Tri-X, meaning bright areas transition gradually to white.

**Overall contrast:** Medium. Less contrast than Tri-X, more than standard medium-contrast films. Suited to contrast adjustment in processing — Acros can be developed to produce high or low contrast depending on developer and development time.

---

## Grain Character

Acros 100's grain is extraordinary. At ISO 100, grain is essentially invisible at normal enlargement — finer than T-Max 100 and significantly finer than any ISO 400 B&W film. At large print sizes, extremely fine, even grain appears — not the chunky organic grain of Tri-X but a fine, regular texture that adds film quality without graphic grain presence.

**Acros as digital simulation:** Fujifilm's Acros film simulation in X-series cameras is based directly on this film stock and is considered one of the best digital B&W simulations available.

---

## Reciprocity

Acros 100 has an unusual and highly valued characteristic: excellent reciprocity failure performance. For long exposures (common in landscape photography), most films require significant additional exposure time as shutter speed decreases below 1 second. Acros 100 requires far less correction, making it ideal for long-exposure photography — waterfalls, architectural interiors, night landscapes with star trails.

## Comparison: Acros 100 vs Kodak Tri-X 400

| Characteristic | Acros 100 | Tri-X 400 |
|----------------|-----------|-----------|
| Grain | Near-invisible | Chunky, organic |
| Contrast | Medium | High |
| Shadow detail | Excellent | Limited by deep blacks |
| Feel | Fine art, precise | Photojournalism, graphic |
| Best for | Landscape, portrait, fine art | Street, documentary |

These two films represent opposite poles of B&W film aesthetics and should produce substantially different prompt results.
