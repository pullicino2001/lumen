# Leica M11 — Camera Profile

**Type:** Digital Rangefinder  
**Sensor:** BSI CMOS, full-frame 35mm (24×36mm)  
**Resolution:** 60MP (full) | 36MP (DNG M) | 18MP (DNG S)  
**Production:** 2022–present  
**ISO Range:** 64–50,000  
**Significance:** Leica's highest-resolution M. Tri-resolution sensor — 60/36/18MP selectable. The current benchmark for digital rangefinder photography.

---

## Visual Identity

The M11 is Leica's most capable and most debated digital M. The 60MP BSI sensor is a different imaging proposition to everything that came before it — resolution is now so high that the limiting factor in most shots is no longer the sensor but the lens, the light, and the photographer's ability to hold still.

The M11's visual character is Leica's most technically refined. Colours are accurate, clean, and rendered with the house colour science that favours warm skin tones and rich reds. But the M11 does not have the M9's romance — it is a precise instrument, and it looks like one. Files are smooth, detailed, and well-behaved. They are less likely to surprise you and more likely to exactly deliver what you composed.

Where the M11 distinguishes itself most is in tonal graduation — the jump from 36MP to 60MP is felt most in mid-tone smoothness, gradient rendering in sky and skin, and the absence of posterisation in subtle tonal transitions. At 60MP, a smooth grey gradient renders with genuine continuity rather than the stepped quality that lower-resolution sensors sometimes produce.

The M11 is also the first M to drop the baseplate for bottom-loading film — it uses a side-door battery access. This is a practical change that some M traditionalists dislike, but it doesn't affect image rendering.

## Colour Science — M11

The M11 builds on the M10's colour science with refinements in the neutral-tone accuracy and highlight-to-white transition.

**Skin tones:** The M11's warmth is more controlled than the M10's. Very slightly warmer than perfectly neutral — but the warmth is applied more precisely, without bleeding into adjacent hues. Olive skin tones are preserved; pale skin tones have warmth without going orange.

**Reds and oranges:** Rich and precise. Leica's reds remain one of the system's strongest characteristics — deeply saturated without clipping, with a quality that feels almost printed rather than captured.

**Blues:** Clean and accurate — more neutral than the M9's cyan blues. Deep blues retain saturation in shadow more than competing CMOS systems.

**Greens and foliage:** Natural and slightly warm. The M11 doesn't push green saturation the way Fuji cameras do. Foliage is faithful rather than vivid.

**Shadow colour:** Neutral, clean. The BSI architecture provides better shadow performance — less shadow noise, less colour contamination at high ISO.

---

## Rendering Character

**Contrast:** Medium-low as captured — the M11's raw files are slightly flat by design, giving maximum latitude for post-processing. Applied contrast via a film stock profile will have more to work with than with older digital cameras.

**Highlights:** Excellent rolloff. The BSI sensor provides the most gradual, film-like highlight rolloff of any Leica M — skies and specular highlights hold detail much longer before clipping.

**Shadows:** Outstanding recovery. ISO 64 base ISO files have enormous shadow headroom — 5+ stops of clean shadow recovery is achievable.

**Sharpness:** Exceptional. At 60MP with a sharp M-mount lens, the M11 resolves fine detail that was simply not capturable with 24MP. Fine fabric weave, individual hair strands, pore-level skin texture, and architectural fine detail are all genuinely captured rather than inferred.

**Micro-contrast:** High. The BSI architecture and lack of anti-aliasing filter produce edge rendition that is extremely crisp without digital sharpening artefacts.

**Noise character:** Fine, luminance-dominant noise at high ISO. At ISO 6400+, noise appears but retains a fine-grain quality that is more organic than earlier digital sensors.

## Tri-Resolution System

The M11's unique feature — three selectable resolution modes in-camera:

| Mode | Resolution | Use Case |
|------|------------|----------|
| Full DNG | 60MP | Maximum detail — architecture, landscapes, studio portraits |
| DNG M | 36MP | Everyday use — plenty of resolution, manageable files |
| DNG S | 18MP | Street, action — faster processing, still excellent quality |

For prompt purposes: the 60MP mode produces a quality approaching medium format. The 18MP mode is approximately equivalent to the M10's quality at reduced file size.

## High ISO Behaviour

| ISO | Character |
|-----|-----------|
| 64–200 | Reference quality. The best the M system offers. |
| 400–800 | Flawless for practical use. |
| 1600–3200 | Excellent. Very fine luminance noise — adds subtle texture. |
| 6400–12500 | Good — fine grain visible. Colour intact. |
| 25000–50000 | Usable — grain is present and honest. Better than most full-frame at extreme ISO. |

## Combination Notes

**M11 + Summilux 50mm f/1.4 + Portra 400:** The definitive modern Leica portrait combination. 60MP resolves every detail the lens can deliver; Portra 400 profile brings warmth and organic colour.

**M11 + Noctilux 50mm f/0.95 + Cinestill 800T:** The night portrait combination. Maximum sensor dynamic range meets the Noctilux's wide-open rendering and Cinestill's characteristic tungsten mood.

**M11 + APO-Summicron 50mm f/2:** The precision test. The APO-Summicron is one of the sharpest lenses ever made — the M11 at 60MP is the first digital M that fully realises its potential.

**M11 + Summicron 35mm f/2 + Kodak Tri-X:** Monochrome street. High-contrast B&W with the M11's excellent shadow/highlight tonal range gives images with genuine print quality.
