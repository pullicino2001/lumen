# Fujifilm X100V / X100VI — Camera Profile

**Type:** Fixed-Lens Compact (APS-C)  
**Sensor:** X100V: 26.1MP X-Trans CMOS IV | X100VI: 40.2MP X-Trans CMOS 5 HR  
**Lens (fixed):** Fujinon 23mm f/2 (equivalent: 35mm on full-frame)  
**Production:** X100V: 2020–2023 | X100VI: 2024–present  
**Film Simulations:** 19 (X100VI) — Provia, Velvia, Astia, Classic Chrome, Pro Neg Hi/Std, Eterna, Classic Neg, Nostalgic Neg, Reala Ace, and more  
**Significance:** The camera that made "the Fuji look" mainstream. Associated with a specific aesthetic of warm, filmic colour processing that became widely imitated.

---

## Visual Identity

The X100 series occupies a unique cultural position in photography. It is not just a camera — it is an aesthetic statement. The X100V/VI's combination of the 35mm-equivalent perspective, APS-C X-Trans sensor, and most importantly Fujifilm's Film Simulation processing produces images that have become immediately recognisable across social media and editorial photography.

The "Fuji look" — as it's known — is characterised by slightly muted but richly tonal colours, a particular handling of skin tones (cool and refined rather than warm and golden), and a filmic tonal curve that has less digital "punch" and more analog "depth." The X100's film simulations, particularly Classic Chrome and Classic Neg, are widely considered some of the most successful JPEG processing profiles in digital photography history.

The fixed 23mm f/2 lens (35mm equivalent) is sharp, compact, and has a particular drawing quality — not as dramatic as any Leica lens, not as clinical as Sony or Nikon, but with a pleasant character that suits the overall Fuji aesthetic.

---

## X-Trans Sensor Character

Fujifilm's X-Trans sensor uses a non-Bayer colour filter array — the pattern of red, green, and blue pixels is randomised rather than repeating in a 2×2 grid. This produces:

- Less moiré interference with fine patterns
- Subtly different colour rendering than Bayer sensors — slightly more complex colour-to-pixel mapping
- A characteristic micro-texture in fine detail areas that some photographers describe as "film-like"
- Historically, some raw processing software has struggled with X-Trans rendering — software-specific artefacts can appear, particularly in foliage

## Film Simulations — Key Profiles

**Provia (Standard):** Neutral, slightly saturated. Clean colour with Fuji's natural bias toward cooler neutrals. Good starting point.

**Classic Chrome:** The most celebrated Film Simulation. Muted, slightly faded colours with compressed highlights and raised shadows. Teal/green shadows, slightly desaturated warm tones. Reads like Kodachrome or a well-processed colour negative. This is the simulation most associated with "the Fuji look."

**Classic Neg:** More saturated than Classic Chrome but with a colour science based on Fuji's Superia consumer negative film. Characteristic green-tinged shadows, natural midtones, slightly compressed highlights. Excellent for street and documentary.

**Velvia/Vivid:** High saturation, high contrast. Landscape and nature — colours are vivid and dramatic. Not suitable for portraits.

**Eterna Cinema:** Low saturation, low contrast, flat rendering designed for video but producing very film-like still images that require post-processing to develop. High latitude.

**Nostalgic Neg:** Added in X100VI. Warm, nostalgic rendering based on Fuji's classic consumer colour negative films. More warmth than Classic Chrome, slight fade.

---

## Colour Science

**Skin tones:** Cooler than Canon or Leica — Fuji's skin tone rendering is more neutral and slightly olive/golden rather than warm-amber. Some photographers prefer this for different skin tones; others find it needs adjustment.

**Greens/foliage:** Fuji's most distinctive characteristic — greens are rich, saturated, and slightly cool (teal rather than yellow-green). Foliage shot on Fuji has an immediately recognisable quality.

**Blues:** Deep and saturated — particularly in Classic Chrome and Classic Neg. Sky blues have great depth.

**Reds:** Slightly muted compared to Canon — Fuji's reds are accurate rather than boosted.

## Combination Notes

**X100V + Classic Chrome:** This combination is the most widely reproduced "Fuji look" in contemporary photography — street, travel, and documentary work with a filmic quality that has influenced a generation of photographers who grew up post-film.

**X100V + Acros:** The Fuji monochrome simulation produces excellent B&W with strong contrast and precise tonal separation. Combined with the 35mm perspective, excellent for street B&W.

**X100VI + Nostalgic Neg:** The warmer, more consumer-nostalgia simulation of the newer camera. Works particularly well for lifestyle and travel imagery.
