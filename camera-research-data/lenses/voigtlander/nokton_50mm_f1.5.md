# Voigtländer Nokton 50mm f/1.5 Aspherical — Lens Profile

**Full name:** Voigtländer Nokton 50mm f/1.5 Aspherical VM  
**Mount:** Leica M (VM = Voigtländer M-mount)  
**Focal length:** 50mm  
**Maximum aperture:** f/1.5  
**Minimum aperture:** f/16  
**Minimum focus distance:** 0.7m  
**Era:** Current version: 2014–present  
**Price point:** Approximately $600–$700 — significantly less expensive than Leica equivalents

---

## Visual Identity

The Voigtländer Nokton 50mm f/1.5 is the most popular non-Leica lens in the Leica M ecosystem. It is made by Cosina in Japan under the Voigtländer brand (a historic German optical brand now produced by Cosina) and is fully compatible with all Leica M cameras.

The Nokton 50mm f/1.5 has a rendering character that is deliberately different from Leica's modern ASPH lenses. Where the Summilux 50 ASPH is technically refined and modern, the Nokton 50 has a slightly softer, more characterful rendering — particularly wide open — that many photographers associate with classic vintage lenses. This "vintage character" is intentional: Voigtländer designed the Nokton to have optical personality.

---

## Rendering Character

**At f/1.5:** Softer than the Summilux at f/1.4. Slight spherical aberration gives subjects a slightly glowing, romantic quality — focus is present but with gentle softening at the edges. This is "character" rather than flaw.

**Bokeh at f/1.5:** Beautiful and distinctive. The Nokton produces a bokeh that has a slight "swirl" — similar to the Noctilux but less extreme. Background highlights have gentle, swirling haloes. This is among the most aesthetically pleasing bokeh of any M-mount lens.

**Colour transmission:** Slightly cooler than Leica Summilux, slightly warmer than Leica Summicron. In the middle. Neutral with a slight cool quality.

**At f/2.8–f/4:** The spherical aberration clears. The Nokton at f/4 is a sharp, clean lens with no major character — performing very similarly to a Leica lens of equivalent specification.

**Vignetting:** Noticeable at f/1.5 — approximately 1.5–2 stops at corners.

## Combination Notes

**On Leica M9 (CCD):** The Nokton's soft, slightly vintage rendering combined with the M9's CCD warmth produces images of extreme analogue romanticism. One of the most distinctive digital photography aesthetics available.

**With Kodak Portra 160:** Fine grain, warm tones, and the Nokton's f/1.5 rendering at portrait distances produces images with great beauty and intimacy.
