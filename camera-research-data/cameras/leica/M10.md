# Leica M10 / M10-R — Camera Profile

**Type:** Digital Rangefinder  
**Sensor:** CMOS, full-frame 35mm (24×36mm)  
**Resolution:** M10: 24MP | M10-R: 40MP  
**Production:** M10: 2017–2020 | M10-R: 2020–2022  
**ISO Range:** 100–50,000  
**Significance:** Leica's CMOS transition done right — the first digital M that feels like shooting the film M cameras.

---

## Visual Identity

The M10 is what happens when Leica finally made a digital rangefinder that didn't fight itself. Previous digital Ms (M8, M9) had the soul but not the practicality. The M11 has the resolution but not the intimacy. The M10 is the sweet spot — a camera that disappears and lets the lens do the work.

Visually, the M10 sits between the M9 and modern clinical digital. It does not have the M9's CCD magic — its colour rendering is more neutral, more recoverable, more accurate. But Leica's colour science is applied with a specific philosophy: warm but not falsely warm, saturated but not aggressively so, with a particular handling of skin tones that prioritises smoothness over accuracy.

The M10-R with 40MP gives the same colour character but with significantly more fine detail — images can be cropped aggressively and still hold up. The added resolution shows in fabric textures, hair detail, and architectural fine grain that the 24MP M10 renders with softer edges.

---

## Colour Science — M10 Series

Leica's CMOS colour science in the M10 generation has a specific signature:

**Skin tones:** Slightly warmer than neutral. A conscious decision — Leica's colour matrix pushes warm-amber into skin tones rather than rendering them cool or clinical.

**Reds and oranges:** Rich and slightly deep. Reds don't blow to hot pink — they have depth. Saturated reds retain detail longer than most digital sensors.

**Blues and teals:** Slightly muted in shadows, more saturated in direct light. Sky blues are clean and pure without the cyan shift of the M9.

**Greens:** Natural, slightly warm. Not the boosted, punchy greens of Fuji — quieter, more organic.

**Neutrals:** Greys and whites are clean. The M10 handles mixed lighting well — less colour cast drift than the M9.

---

## Rendering Character

**Contrast:** Medium. The M10's default JPEG rendering (and its RAW response) has a more neutral contrast curve than the M9. Tones are evenly distributed. It rewards careful post-processing or — in the context of Lumen — a well-calibrated LUT.

**Highlights:** Gradual rolloff. The M10 recovers highlights better than the M9 — blown sky can often be pulled back. This is a CMOS advantage.

**Shadows:** Excellent shadow recovery. Low noise at ISO 400–800. The M10's CMOS allows shadow lifting without the colour cross-contamination of the M9 at high ISO.

**Sharpness:** High out of camera, especially with Leica's M-mount lenses. The lack of anti-aliasing filter (standard in Leica M cameras) means micro-contrast is strong — fine detail is resolved with crispness.

**Texture:** Smooth at base ISO. Noise at ISO 3200–6400 is fine and luminance-dominant — it adds texture without damaging colour.

## M10 vs M10-R

| Characteristic | M10 (24MP) | M10-R (40MP) |
|----------------|------------|--------------|
| Fine detail | Good | Excellent |
| Colour rendering | Identical | Identical |
| High ISO | Slightly better (larger pixels) | Slightly more noise at extreme ISO |
| File size | Manageable | Large — storage/processing demands |
| Recommended use | Street, everyday | Architecture, landscapes, studio portrait |

## M10-R Addendum

The M10-R's additional resolution produces a specific quality in fine-textured subjects: fabric weave, skin pores, hair, stone. At 40MP, these subjects have an almost medium-format quality — detail is recorded, not inferred. When combined with Leica's sharpest lenses (APO-Summicron, Summilux ASPH), the result is files that hold up to significant output enlargement.

**M10-R Prompt Fragment addendum:**
```
high-resolution 40MP rendering, exceptional fine-detail resolution approaching
medium-format quality, fine texture rendered with precision rather than suggestion
```

## Combination Notes

**M10 + Summilux 50mm f/1.4 + Portra 400:** The "warm Leica digital" combination. Clean, modern files with analogue colour character via the Portra profile.

**M10 + Summicron 35mm f/2 + Tri-X:** The documentary combination. High contrast monochrome with excellent shadow detail that the M10's sensor provides.

**M10-R + APO-Summicron 50mm f/2:** The precision combination. Technically demanding but produces astonishing results — the full 40MP is realised only with the sharpest lenses.
