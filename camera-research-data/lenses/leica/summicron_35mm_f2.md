# Leica Summicron-M 35mm f/2 ASPH — Lens Profile

**Full name:** Leica Summicron-M 35mm f/2 ASPH  
**Mount:** Leica M  
**Focal length:** 35mm  
**Maximum aperture:** f/2  
**Minimum aperture:** f/16  
**Minimum focus distance:** 0.7m  
**Construction:** 7 elements in 5 groups, 1 aspherical element  
**Era:** 1997–present  
**Previous versions:** Pre-ASPH versions from 1958 (8 elements), multiple generations  

---

## Visual Identity

The 35mm focal length on a Leica M is the defining combination. More photographs considered masterpieces of street, documentary, and reportage photography were made at 35mm on an M camera than at any other focal length. The Summicron 35mm ASPH is the modern realisation of this lineage.

The 35mm perspective sits between normal (50mm) and moderate wide-angle (28mm). It captures more of the scene than a 50mm — backgrounds are included, context is present — but without the environmental dominance of a wider lens. At the same time, close-focus work at 35mm is intimate in a way that 50mm cannot achieve without backing up. The subject is in the world; the world is around the subject.

The Summicron 35 ASPH is a precise, clean lens that renders with slightly more warmth than the Summicron 50 — a characteristic of the ASPH design and coating. It is sharper in the corners at wide apertures than any pre-ASPH version, which makes it excellent for architecture and landscapes alongside its traditional street use.

## Aperture by Aperture

### f/2 — Wide Open
**Character:** Very good wide open — the ASPH element significantly improves corner performance compared to older 35mm Leica lenses. Centre sharpness is high. Corners are slightly soft but not unusable. Chromatic aberration is minimal.

**Vignetting:** Moderate — approximately 1.5 stops at corners. Creates natural frame emphasis without being heavy.

**35mm depth of field at f/2:** Noticeably more than 50mm at f/2. At 1.5m, approximately 10cm depth of field. The subject is isolated but not radically — some of the environment remains in focus. This is the "wide-open 35mm portrait" look: subject-in-context.

**Bokeh:** Pleasant but not the Summilux's creaminess. The 35mm focal length means out-of-focus areas are smaller relative to frame than at 50mm. Bokeh is smooth rather than dramatic.

---

### f/2.8–f/4 — Excellent
Near-perfect performance. Corners sharpen to match the centre. The Summicron 35 at f/4 is one of the finest wide-angle lenses for street and documentary work.

---

### f/5.6–f/8 — Optimal
Full-frame uniformity. Maximum performance.

## Optical Character

**Perspective at 35mm:** The 35mm focal length is considered by many photographers to be the most "human" wide angle — it captures how we see when we are paying attention to a wider scene. Not as extreme as a 28mm but with more environmental context than a 50mm. Street photography at 35mm allows working at approximately arm's length for half-body portraits — closer to subjects, more direct, more intimate than the 50mm distance.

**3D quality:** The Summicron 35 ASPH has a pronounced three-dimensional rendering despite its relatively modest maximum aperture. The ASPH element produces a drawing that separates the focus plane from surrounding planes with micro-contrast. Objects at different distances appear to have spatial depth.

**Close-focus performance:** The Summicron 35 excels at close focus — minimum 0.7m. Close portraits at 35mm have an environmental quality with some natural perspective distortion (noses appear slightly larger, backgrounds slightly wider) that adds vitality and energy to the image.

**Colour transmission:** Slightly warm — warmer than the Summicron 50. The 35mm Summicron adds a subtle golden quality to midtone colours, particularly in skin and foliage. Not dramatically warm — perceptible rather than obvious.

## Combination Notes

**The classic street combination:** M6 (or M body) + Summicron 35mm f/2 + Kodak Tri-X 400. Henri Cartier-Bresson, later converted to 50mm, but Garry Winogrand, Diane Arbus, and countless photojournalists used 35mm. This combination produces the archetypal street photograph aesthetic.

**With Kodak Portra 400:** Environmental portraits. The 35mm perspective includes more of the person's surroundings than a 50mm would — useful for portraits that tell stories about people's lives and places.

**With Fuji Superia 400:** The slight green cast of Superia combined with the Summicron 35's slight warmth creates an interesting tension — neutral but with colour character. Good for available-light indoor work.
