# Ilford Delta 400 Professional — Film Stock Profile

**Type:** Black and White Negative (T-grain / tabular grain)  
**ISO:** 400  
**Format:** 35mm, 120  
**Production:** 1990–present  
**Significance:** The finest-grain ISO 400 B&W film. The modern, refined alternative to HP5.

---

## Visual Identity

Ilford Delta 400 uses tabular (T-grain) crystal technology — the same crystal shape used in Kodak T-Max films. T-grain crystals are flatter and more uniformly oriented than conventional cubic crystals, producing a finer, more regular grain structure for a given film speed.

Delta 400 is the technically refined choice among Ilford's ISO 400 films. Where HP5 Plus has organic, slightly random grain character and a warmer tonal rendering, Delta 400 is cooler, more precise, and more modern in character. Images from Delta 400 have a quality that bridges film and digital — fine enough grain to retain digital-quality apparent sharpness, but with the tonal depth and rendering quality of traditional B&W chemistry.

---

## Tonal Response

**Shadows:** Excellent detail retention. Delta's tabular grain produces better shadow tonality than conventional crystal films — shadow regions have more visible tonal separation.

**Midtones:** Precise and slightly analytical — less "organic" than HP5 but with excellent tonal separation. Skin tones are rendered with careful precision.

**Highlights:** Gradual rolloff — comparable to Acros 100 in highlight handling despite being ISO 400.

**Overall contrast:** Medium. Versatile — can be processed to produce high or low contrast. The default (D-76 processing) gives a medium-contrast result with the full tonal range.

---

## Grain Character

Finer and more regular than HP5 Plus. At ISO 400, grain is present but refined — adding film quality without graphic grain impact. At ISO 1600 (push), grain becomes visible but remains more regular and refined than HP5 or Tri-X pushed to the same speed.
