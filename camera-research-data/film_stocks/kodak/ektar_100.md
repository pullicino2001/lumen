# Kodak Ektar 100 — Film Stock Profile

**Type:** Colour Negative (C-41 process)  
**ISO:** 100  
**Format:** 35mm, 120  
**Production:** Original Ektar 1989, reformulated as current Ektar 100: 2008–present  
**Significance:** The world's finest-grain colour negative film. Designed for maximum saturation, maximum sharpness, and minimum grain.

---

## Visual Identity

Ektar 100 is the anti-Portra. Where Portra is warm, forgiving, and skin-flattering, Ektar is cool, precise, and unforgiving. Ektar wants subjects with saturated colours — landscapes, architecture, product photography, flowers, cars. It does not want skin tones. Ektar renders caucasian skin with a slightly orange-red cast that most portrait photographers find unflattering.

But pointed at the right subjects, Ektar produces photographs with a quality unlike any other colour negative film. The T-grain (tabular grain) technology creates a grain structure so fine it approaches the apparent resolution of medium-format film in a 35mm frame. Colours are rendered with a vivid precision — not the warm richness of Portra, but a cool, jewel-like quality.

---

## Colour Rendering

**Blues:** Ektar's strongest characteristic. Sky blue, water, turquoise — Ektar renders blues with extraordinary saturation and depth. A clear blue sky on Ektar looks like a saturated magazine photograph.

**Reds:** Very saturated. This is the source of Ektar's skin tone problem — reds are too vivid for natural skin rendering, but extraordinary for flowers, vehicles, and architecture.

**Greens:** Cool and vivid. Not Fuji's warm-teal green but a cool, saturated botanical green. Excellent for nature and garden photography.

**Shadows:** Cool — Ektar's shadows lean blue-purple compared to Portra's warm shadows. This gives Ektar images a different atmospheric quality — not warm evenings but bright cool mornings.

**Neutrals:** Slightly cool — Ektar's whites are clean and bright, its greys lean slightly cold.

---

## Grain

Ektar 100 has the finest grain of any commonly available colour negative film. The T-grain technology produces apparent resolution approaching ISO 25–50 stocks from the film era. At normal enlargement, grain is essentially invisible. At very large print sizes, extremely fine regular grain appears.

## Best Use Cases

Landscape, architecture, travel, automotive, botanical/macro, product photography. Any subject dominated by colour rather than skin tones.
