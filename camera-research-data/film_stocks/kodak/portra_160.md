# Kodak Portra 160 — Film Stock Profile

**Type:** Colour Negative (C-41 process)  
**ISO:** 160  
**Format:** 35mm, 120, 4×5  
**Production:** 1998–present (reformulated 2010 with Portra 400)  
**Significance:** Portra 400's finer-grain sibling. The ultimate portrait film in ideal conditions.

---

## Visual Identity

Portra 160 shares Portra 400's colour science — the same warm skin tones, the same rich reds, the same generous exposure latitude. The difference is grain. At ISO 160, Portra's grain is significantly finer — almost invisible at 35mm, extremely fine at 120. When conditions allow the slower speed (studio, controlled light, tripod, bright daylight), Portra 160 produces the finest-grain colour negative images available.

---

## Colour Rendering

Identical to Portra 400 in colour science and tonal response. The same:
- Warm amber-golden skin tones
- Rich reds with depth and detail
- Slightly muted blues
- Warm midtone bias
- Gradual highlight rolloff

The primary difference from Portra 400:

**Grain:** Significantly finer. Near-invisible at 35mm in normal viewing conditions. Extremely fine in 120 format — approaching the apparent resolution of medium-format at full frame.

**Exposure latitude:** Slightly less than Portra 400 — the slower ISO film has slightly less forgiveness for overexposure. But still generous by film standards.

---

## When to Use vs Portra 400

**Portra 160:** Studio portraits, weddings with good light, controlled outdoor portraits, medium-format work where maximum image quality is desired.

**Portra 400:** Available light, uncertain light, everyday shooting, situations requiring faster shutter speeds or smaller apertures.
