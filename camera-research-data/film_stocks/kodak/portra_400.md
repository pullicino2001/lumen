# Kodak Portra 400 — Film Stock Profile

**Type:** Colour Negative (C-41 process)  
**ISO:** 400  
**Format:** 35mm, 120, 4×5  
**Production:** 1998–present (reformulated 2010)  
**Significance:** The world's most widely used professional portrait film. The benchmark for colour negative photography.

---

## Visual Identity

Kodak Portra 400 is the gold standard of colour negative film for a reason that is difficult to reduce to technical specifications: it makes skin look beautiful. The colour science embedded in Portra's dye layers was specifically designed by Kodak's engineers to render human skin tones with warmth, smoothness, and a naturalness that no digital camera or post-processing workflow has fully replicated.

The 2010 reformulation of Portra 400 improved on an already-excellent stock. The grain became finer, the highlight rolloff became smoother, and the colour rendering became more consistent across a wider range of lighting conditions. The result is a film that is simultaneously forgiving (excellent exposure latitude, 5+ stops) and exquisitely rendered when properly exposed.

---

## Colour Rendering

**Skin tones:** Portra's primary virtue. Warm amber-to-orange tones in Caucasian skin, rich and precise rendering of melanated skin, smooth transitions in shadow and highlight areas of the face. The warmth is built into the film's dye layers — it is not yellow or orange, but a specific golden warmth that translates directly to human attractiveness in photographs.

**Reds and oranges:** Rich and deep. Saturated without clipping. Roses, lips, autumn leaves — Portra's reds have extraordinary depth and remain detailed even when strongly exposed.

**Greens:** Natural, slightly warm. Not the cool-teal of Fuji negative films. Foliage has a warmth that reads as "summer afternoon" rather than "spring morning."

**Blues and skies:** Slightly muted compared to Fuji or slide films — Portra's blues are deep but not vivid. Sky blues have a natural, photographic quality without the artificiality of over-saturated digital sky.

**Neutrals:** Slightly warm. Whites have a gentle cream quality; greys are warm rather than cold. This consistency means Portra looks good in mixed lighting without colour casts becoming problematic.

---

## Tonal Response

**Shadow:** Deep and rich. Portra's shadows retain colour and detail — they do not go muddy or lose chroma. Shadow regions have a specific quality: darker than reality, but with all the colour information preserved.

**Midtones:** This is where Portra lives. Rich, smooth, with that characteristic warmth. The midtone rendering is the core of the Portra look — faces, fabric, skin, the ordinary tones of the world rendered with exceptional care.

**Highlights:** The reformulated 2010 Portra 400 has exceptionally gradual highlight rolloff — one of the most film-like highlight transitions of any colour negative stock. Overexposed highlights transition gradually to white through a warm, slightly golden blown-out zone rather than abruptly clipping. This forgives overexposure and produces naturalistic results.

---

## Grain

At ISO 400, Portra's grain is remarkably fine — finer than Fuji Superia 400, significantly finer than Kodak Ultramax 400. Pushed to ISO 800 (one stop over), grain becomes visible but organic. At ISO 1600 (two stops over), grain is present and adds character without destroying image quality.

The grain structure is the classic Kodak silver-halide grain: slightly chunky compared to tabular grain stocks (T-Max), with a tendency to cluster in shadow regions (shadow-weighted grain). The result reads as "film grain" to any observer — not noise, not texture, but genuine photographic grain.

---

## Exposure Latitude

Portra 400 has outstanding exposure latitude — arguably the best of any colour negative film:
- Underexpose by 1 stop: colours become slightly more saturated, shadows go deeper, but image quality remains excellent
- Overexpose by 2–3 stops: highlights roll off gradually, colours become lighter and slightly pastel, grain becomes effectively invisible
- Overexpose by 4+ stops: image begins to lose density — very light, almost translucent feel with pastel colours

The 2010 reformulation significantly improved highlight latitude specifically. Photographers commonly "rate" Portra at ISO 200 (one stop overexpose) for maximum shadow detail and highlight latitude.

## Interaction with Lenses

**With Leica Summilux 50mm f/1.4:** The classic portrait combination. Summilux warmth + Portra warmth is complementary, not conflicting. Skin tones become particularly beautiful.

**With Leica Noctilux 50mm f/0.95:** Wide open, the thin depth of field isolates the subject in Portra's warm rendering — the rest of the frame becomes a warm, smooth melt.

**With Canon 85mm f/1.2L:** The Canon's inherent warmth combined with Portra's creates an extremely warm, romantic portrait aesthetic — popular but easy to oversaturate. Careful balance required.
