# Ilford HP5 Plus 400 — Film Stock Profile

**Type:** Black and White Negative  
**ISO:** 400 (excellent pushability to 800, 1600, 3200)  
**Format:** 35mm, 120, 4×5 and other sheet film sizes  
**Production:** HP5: 1976 | HP5 Plus: 1989–present  
**Significance:** Britain's most important film stock. The accessible, versatile B&W film — the thinking person's Tri-X.

---

## Visual Identity

HP5 Plus is often compared to Kodak Tri-X because they occupy the same position — ISO 400 B&W negative, excellent pushability, photojournalistic character. But they are distinctly different. Where Tri-X is American — bold, high-contrast, graphic, authoritative — HP5 is British — refined, tonally nuanced, slightly more subtle. HP5 has a different temperament.

HP5's rendering has a quality of tonal subtlety that Tri-X does not. Shadow areas are less aggressively dark — more retained detail. Midtones are richer and more differentiated. Highlights roll off more gracefully. The result is images that feel less graphically extreme but more tonally complete — every zone of the grey scale is present and well-rendered.

HP5 is beloved by photographers who want the versatility and grain character of an ISO 400 B&W film but with more tonal range and less of Tri-X's aggressive contrast. Architectural photographers particularly appreciate HP5's even shadow and highlight handling.

---

## Tonal Response

**Shadows:** Better shadow detail retention than Tri-X at equivalent exposure. Shadows are dark but not as aggressively black. Shadow-weighted grain adds texture without completely obscuring detail.

**Midtones:** Rich and full. HP5's midtone rendering is perhaps its strongest quality — faces, fabric, stone, wood all rendered with the classic British B&W printing quality. Think Ansel Adams but with more personality.

**Highlights:** Gradual rolloff — better than Tri-X. Overexposed highlight areas retain detail longer before clipping to white.

**Overall contrast:** Medium-high. Less extreme than Tri-X. More flexible across different lighting conditions.

---

## Grain Character

HP5's grain is similar to Tri-X in size but slightly different in character — less chunky, slightly more regular. At ISO 400, grain is visible and characteristic — adding the filmic quality expected from an ISO 400 B&W stock. Shadow grain is pronounced; midtone grain is moderate; highlight grain is minimal.

**Pushed to ISO 1600:** HP5 is an excellent push film — better than Tri-X by many photographers' accounts. At ISO 1600, grain becomes heavy but remains organic and aesthetically pleasing rather than harsh. High contrast and substantial grain with excellent tonal separation.

**Pushed to ISO 3200:** Extreme grain, very high contrast. Deep documentary quality. Less common than 1600 push but used for very low-light available-light work.
