# Leica Noctilux-M 50mm f/0.95 ASPH — Lens Profile

**Full name:** Leica Noctilux-M 50mm f/0.95 ASPH  
**Mount:** Leica M  
**Focal length:** 50mm  
**Maximum aperture:** f/0.95  
**Minimum aperture:** f/16  
**Minimum focus distance:** 1.0m  
**Construction:** 10 elements in 6 groups, 2 aspherical elements  
**Era:** 2008–present  
**Previous versions:** Noctilux 50mm f/1.0 (1975–2008), Noctilux 50mm f/1.2 (1966–1975)  
**Weight:** 700g (the heaviest M-mount lens)

---

## Visual Identity

The Noctilux is not a normal lens. It is a statement — a technical and artistic extreme. At f/0.95, it is the fastest lens in regular production for a full-frame camera, and it renders in a way that is unlike anything else. Wide open, the Noctilux dissolves the world. Focus is precise and thin — at 1 metre, the depth of field at f/0.95 is measured in millimetres. Everything not at the exact focus distance becomes a swirling, impressionistic melt.

The experience of shooting the Noctilux wide open is often described as "dreamy" — a word that does not adequately capture the severity of the effect. Images shot at f/0.95 have an almost hallucinogenic quality: the sharp eye in a portrait while the rest of the face already dissolves, street lights becoming elongated glowing discs, dark corridors where light pools on faces while walls disappear into darkness. It is optically extreme and deliberately so.

This is not a reportage lens. It is not a documentary lens. It is a vision — sometimes used to extraordinary effect by photographers who know exactly what they are doing with it.

## Aperture by Aperture

### f/0.95 — Maximum Aperture
**Character:** Extreme. Depth of field at any normal portrait distance is so thin that precise focus is the central challenge. What is sharp is *sharp* — but the transition to out-of-focus begins almost immediately. The in-focus zone has a quality unique to this lens: high micro-contrast with a slight luminous glow at edges.

**Vignetting:** Very strong — approximately 3 stops at the corners. The frame edges darken dramatically, creating a natural spotlight effect. The centre is illuminated; the periphery dissolves into shadow.

**Chromatic aberration:** Present and characteristic. Warm and cool fringing at high-contrast edges adds to the impressionistic quality. This is not correctable without destroying the rendering.

**Bokeh:** The Noctilux's wide-open bokeh is its defining characteristic. Out-of-focus highlights — point light sources, reflections, catchlights — become large, softly glowing discs with gentle swirling in their centres. This "swirl" is a known optical characteristic of the lens design.

**Focus precision required:** At f/0.95, rangefinder focus must be perfect. Even a slight miss renders subjects unrecognisably soft. The Noctilux demands — and rewards — exacting technique.

**Use cases:** Low-light available light work where nothing else is fast enough. Night photography with ambient light only. Deliberately atmospheric portraiture. Conceptual or artistic photography.

---

### f/1.4 — Still Exceptional
**Character:** The swirling bokeh settles. Vignetting reduces. Chromatic aberration significantly reduced. But depth of field remains very thin — this is still an extreme aperture. The impressionistic quality diminishes and a more purposeful rendering emerges. Often considered the best balance for portraits — extreme subject isolation, reduced vignette, cleaner subject rendering.

---

### f/2–f/2.8 — Good Lens
**Character:** By f/2, the Noctilux is a very good 50mm lens — sharp, with pleasant bokeh and controlled rendering. But it is no longer exceptional. At this aperture, the Summilux 50 or Summicron 50 produce comparable results.

---

### f/4 and above — Standard Performance
At f/4–f/8, the Noctilux is sharp corner to corner and renders well. But there is no optical reason to use the Noctilux at these apertures over a smaller, lighter lens.

---

## Optical Character

**The f/0.95 depth of field:** At 1.5m focusing distance (typical portrait distance), the depth of field at f/0.95 is approximately 2cm. At f/1.4 it is approximately 3.5cm. This means that on a portrait, if the eyes are in focus, the tip of the nose and the ears may already be outside the depth of field.

**Vignetting as creative tool:** The Noctilux's wide-open vignetting is so strong that it effectively acts as an in-lens spotlight. Faces are brighter; backgrounds darken. This creates a natural sense of depth and atmosphere that no post-processing can replicate.

**The "glow":** Wide open, the Noctilux produces a slight glow around bright subjects — a luminous fringe at the transition between sharp and soft. This is not flare; it is residual spherical aberration in the design. Some photographers use this specifically: photographing people in backlit situations or against bright windows, where the glow halos their outline.

**Night light rendering:** Street lights, lamps, candles, and window light at night become glowing discs when out of focus. Close to the focus distance, they are sharp points of light. At distance, they become large, soft, beautifully rendered bokeh balls. Night street scenes shot at f/0.95 have an almost painted quality.

## Combination Notes

**With Leica M11 (60MP):** The paradox of maximum resolution meeting maximum impressionism. At f/0.95, the M11's 60MP records everything at the focus plane with extraordinary precision — and everything else becomes abstract.

**With Cinestill 800T:** Night street with the Noctilux and Cinestill produces the most distinctive "cinematic night" aesthetic in photography. Tungsten sources become large warm orange discs; the rem-jet effect adds colour bleeding around highlights; the Noctilux's vignetting frames each image like a cinema close-up.

**With Kodak Tri-X (pushed to 3200):** Available-light B&W. The Noctilux's tonal rendering in monochrome is extremely dramatic — subjects caught in available light, deep shadow, grain throughout. Photojournalistic intensity.

## A Note on Technique

The Noctilux at f/0.95 is a technically demanding lens on a rangefinder. The combination of thin depth of field and rangefinder patch focus means that even a small calibration error in the camera will produce missed focus. Many Noctilux users have their M cameras precision-calibrated to the specific lens. When technique and calibration are correct, the results are unlike anything else in photography. When they're not, the results are simply blurry.
