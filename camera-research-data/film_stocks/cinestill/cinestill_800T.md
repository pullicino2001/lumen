# Cinestill 800T — Film Stock Profile

**Type:** Colour Negative (C-41 process)  
**ISO:** 800 (tungsten-balanced)  
**Format:** 35mm  
**Production:** 2012–present  
**Origin:** Kodak Vision3 500T motion picture film, rem-jet backing removed and respooled for C-41  
**Significance:** The most distinctive-looking colour film of the modern era. The "neon night city" film.

---

## Visual Identity

Cinestill 800T is unique in the film world. It is a motion picture film (Kodak Vision3 500T) that has had its rem-jet anti-halation backing removed so it can be processed in standard C-41 colour negative chemistry. The removal of the rem-jet backing is the source of Cinestill's signature characteristic: **halation**.

In normal film, the rem-jet backing absorbs light that passes through the emulsion, preventing it from reflecting back up through the film and creating a secondary exposure around bright light sources. Without this backing, bright light sources — street lights, neon signs, headlights, candles, windows at night — reflect back through the emulsion and create a characteristic warm red/orange glow around themselves. This **halation** is the Cinestill look.

The film is also tungsten-balanced (the "T" in 800T), meaning it is calibrated for artificial tungsten light. In daylight, this produces a strong cool-blue cast. Under tungsten light (incandescent, orange street lights), it renders colour more accurately. Under neon or LED, the tungsten balance interacts unpredictably and fascinatingly.

---

## Colour Rendering

**Under tungsten light:** Relatively accurate colour — this is the film's designed condition. Warm artificial light renders naturally. Skin tones are fair to good.

**Under daylight:** Strong cool-blue cast. Blues and teals dominate. Warm tones are suppressed. The image has a cold, cinematic quality.

**Shadows:** Deep blue-teal. Cinestill's shadows are one of its defining characteristics — under any light, shadow areas shift toward cool blue-purple, creating the distinctive "night city" shadow quality.

**Highlights around light sources:** The halation effect. Warm orange-red glow surrounding any bright point or area light source. The intensity of the halation depends on the brightness and size of the light source and how overexposed the area around it is. Small, very bright sources (neon signs, street lamps) produce the most dramatic halation.

**Skin tones under mixed light:** Complex. Under daylight: too cool. Under tungsten: reasonable. Under mixed (tungsten + some daylight or neon): interesting and unpredictable.

---

## Tonal Response

**Contrast:** Medium. Cinestill's base contrast (it is a motion picture stock) is moderate — wide tonal range, good shadow and highlight detail. The dramatic appearance in most Cinestill images comes from the halation and colour, not from extreme contrast.

**Shadow detail:** Good. Vision3's underlying emulsion has a motion-picture quality of shadow rendering — detail is retained in dark areas.

**Highlights:** Good rolloff in normal areas. Light sources themselves overflow into halation rather than simply clipping.

---

## Grain

At ISO 800, grain is moderate — finer than you would expect at this speed because Vision3 500T was designed for low-grain cinematic use. Grain has a slightly different character to still-photography film — finer and more regular in midtones, with shadow noise more visible.

## Cinestill 400D (comparison)

Cinestill 400D (2021, based on Kodak Vision3 250D) is the daylight-balanced counterpart. Under daylight, it produces accurate, slightly filmic colour without the strong blue cast of 800T. Still has halation from the removed rem-jet backing — but with smaller, slightly cooler halos (the film is daylight-balanced, so the halation colour is less warm).

---

## Cultural Significance

Cinestill 800T became the film associated with a specific aesthetic movement in the 2010s: urban night photography with neon and available light. The look — deep blue shadows, warm orange halation, cinematic colour — was widely adopted on social media and influenced the colour grading of major films and television programmes. It is one of the most immediately recognisable film aesthetics of the decade.
