# Leica Summicron-M 50mm f/2 — Lens Profile

**Full name:** Leica Summicron-M 50mm f/2 (current version — 6th generation)  
**Mount:** Leica M  
**Focal length:** 50mm  
**Maximum aperture:** f/2  
**Minimum aperture:** f/16  
**Minimum focus distance:** 0.7m  
**Construction:** 6 elements in 4 groups  
**Era:** Current version: 1979–present (largely unchanged in 45+ years)  
**Previous versions:** 1st-5th generations, 1954–1979  

---

## Visual Identity

If the Summilux 50 f/1.4 is Leica's soul, the Summicron 50 f/2 is its conscience. This lens has been in continuous production, essentially unchanged, for over 45 years — because Leica found a formula that works and saw no reason to improve it. That formula is: maximum sharpness for a 50mm f/2, clean colour transmission, controlled rendering without personality quirks, and a size small enough to disappear on the camera.

The Summicron 50 does not do anything dramatic. It does not produce the Summilux's painterly bokeh or the Noctilux's dreamy impressionism. What it does is render the world with exceptional clarity, accurate colour, and a micro-contrast that makes digital files from this lens look almost like they were scanned from fine-grain medium-format film — clean, crisp, and with a particular bite at the point of focus.

For documentary photography, photojournalism, and any work where precision and reliability matter more than atmosphere, the Summicron 50 is the correct choice. It is the lens Sebastião Salgado uses. The lens William Eggleston reportedly used. The standard by which all other standard lenses are measured — and which most fail to match.

## Aperture by Aperture

### f/2 — Wide Open
**Character:** Excellent. Unlike the Summilux or Noctilux, the Summicron is outstanding *at its maximum aperture*. This is its defining characteristic: there is no "sacrifice" wide open. Sharpness is high, chromatic aberration is extremely well-controlled, and the rendering is clean and purposeful.

**Vignetting:** Moderate — approximately 1 stop at the corners. Enough to subtly focus attention on the centre of the frame.

**Depth of field:** At f/2 and 1.5m, approximately 5cm. Noticeably more depth of field than the Summilux at f/1.4 — images have more context, less radical subject isolation.

**Bokeh:** Pleasant and smooth but not exceptional. The Summicron produces acceptable out-of-focus areas without the Summilux's creamy background dissolution. Bokeh circles are well-defined but not glamorous.

**Best for:** Available-light documentary, street photography, indoor work in moderate light.

---

### f/2.8–f/4 — Excellent
Approaching peak performance. Vignetting largely gone. Sharpness is very high. A clean, authoritative rendering — whatever the Summicron points at, it renders with precision.

---

### f/5.6–f/8 — Optimal
Maximum performance. Diffraction not yet a factor. Corner-to-corner uniformity. The Summicron at f/5.6–f/8 is a benchmark for optical performance.

## Optical Character

**Micro-contrast:** The Summicron 50's strongest attribute. Fine texture — fabric weave, skin pores, the grain of stone walls, the veins of leaves — is resolved with a crispness that makes subjects appear hyper-real rather than photographed. This micro-contrast is the "bite" that Summicron users describe: a quality of edge transitions that makes the image feel three-dimensional even in print.

**Colour neutrality:** The Summicron is among the most colour-neutral lenses in the M system. It adds no warmth, no cool, no saturation enhancement. What the camera's sensor sees, the Summicron delivers faithfully. This makes it the best lens for accurate colour work — fashion, product, documentary.

**Contrast:** High. The Summicron has a characteristic snap to its rendering — deep blacks, crisp highlights, tonal separation that gives images a sense of depth without manipulation.

**Rendering predictability:** The Summicron is entirely consistent. Every frame is rendered the same way. There are no aperture surprises, no focus-distance rendering changes, no conditions where it behaves unexpectedly. For professionals who need to know what they will get, this reliability is invaluable.

---

## The 50mm Perspective

The 50mm focal length approximately matches human single-eye vision. Images at 50mm have a natural, non-dramatic perspective — subjects are rendered at approximately their true proportions and with spatial relationships that mirror what the eye sees. There is no compression from telephoto, no expansion from wide angle. The Summicron 50 represents the world as it is.

This naturalness is both the strength and the limitation. Summicron 50 images rarely feel dramatic or stylised. They feel honest. This is exactly what documentary and photojournalistic work demands.

## Combination Notes

**With Kodak Tri-X:** The classic photojournalism pairing. The Summicron's high contrast combined with Tri-X's grain produces images of documentary authenticity — the look of Magnum photographers in the 1960s–1980s.

**With Leica M9 (CCD):** The CCD colour adds warmth and depth to the Summicron's neutral rendering. The combination produces a distinctive quality: analytically precise detail but with an analogue warmth that prevents it from feeling clinical.

**With Kodak Portra 400:** Clean, accurate portrait work. The Summicron's colour neutrality allows Portra's warmth to dominate without the lens adding additional colour cast. Clean skin tones, accurate colours, precise focus.

## Versus APO-Summicron 50mm f/2

The APO-Summicron (see separate profile) is the Summicron's resolution-optimised successor for digital M cameras. At f/2, the APO is sharper and has better controlled chromatic aberration. However:

- The APO-Summicron produces a rendering that some describe as "too perfect" — clinical and analytical rather than expressive
- The standard Summicron has a rendering that some prefer for its slightly warmer, more organic quality
- The standard Summicron is significantly smaller and lighter
- The standard Summicron is significantly less expensive

For film or the M9 (CCD), the standard Summicron may actually be the preferred choice — the CCD adds character that the APO's perfection does not need to compensate for.
