# Leica M6 — Camera Profile

**Type:** 35mm Film Rangefinder  
**Production:** 1984–2002, reissued 2022  
**Format:** 35mm film (24×36mm frame)  
**Metering:** TTL centre-weighted light meter (no AE — fully manual)

---

## Visual Identity

The M6 is not a rendering engine — it is a mechanical frame. Its visual output is entirely defined by the lens mounted and the film loaded. What the M6 contributes is the *discipline* of shooting: there is no motor drive, no burst mode, no autofocus hesitation. Every frame is deliberate. This deliberateness shows up in the photographs — composed with intent, unhurried, slightly removed from the moment rather than buried inside it.

The M6's mechanical silence and minimal vibration mean images are exceptionally sharp at slow shutter speeds by hand. No mirror slap. No shutter shock. The only camera movement is you.

Film shot through an M6 is processed normally — the rendering is 100% the film stock's domain. What the M6 adds is the *rangefinder perspective*: images taken from a distance slightly greater than the scene, with awareness of what is just outside the frame (the M6's viewfinder shows the world beyond the frame lines, unlike an SLR). This tends to produce images with a certain compositional space and breathing room.

---

## Sensor / Film Plane

- Standard 35mm negative: 24mm × 36mm
- No in-camera processing — rendering is entirely film + development
- Double-stroke film advance (later models: single stroke)
- Self-timer available but rarely used
- ISO range: 25–6400 (DX coding or manual set)

---

## Rendering Character

Since the M6 outputs to film, its "rendering" is the sum of lens + film stock. However, some notes on the camera's influence:

**Optical path:** The M6's viewfinder is a separate optical system from the taking lens — you see the world through a bright finder window, not through the lens itself. This means what you see in the viewfinder is not exactly what you'll get on film. Experienced M6 shooters develop an intuition for this distance.

**Shutter vibration:** Near zero compared to SLR cameras. This allows hand-held shooting at 1/15s–1/30s with consistently sharp results, particularly with the grain of faster films absorbing any micro-movement.

**No autofocus hunting:** The rangefinder patch shows exact focus confirmation. No hunting, no miss, no compromise. The result is precise subject sharpness exactly where intended.

---

## Tonal Response

Entirely film-dependent. See individual film stock profiles. However, the M6's lack of any in-camera processing means:
- No sharpening applied
- No noise reduction applied
- No colour matrix applied
- What the film captures is what you develop

The M6 is the purest expression of the lens + film combination. It adds nothing except a precisely-exposed frame.

---

## Typical Shooting Style

- Manual focus via rangefinder patch (centre of viewfinder)
- Manual exposure — aperture and shutter speed set by hand
- Typically shot with fast primes: 35mm or 50mm
- Often pre-focused ("zone focus") for street photography
- Single frame, considered composition, minimal chimp behaviour

## Combination Notes

The M6 + Summicron 35mm f/2 + Kodak Tri-X is one of the most iconic combinations in street photography. Henri Cartier-Bresson, Daido Moriyama, and countless photojournalists used this or near-equivalent setups. The combination produces images with high contrast, strong grain, and a particular hardness of light that has defined how "documentary photography" looks to most people.

The M6 + Summilux 50mm f/1.4 + Kodak Portra 400 is the portrait shooter's go-to. The wide aperture, precise rangefinder focus, and Portra's skin-tone rendering produce something that is technically reproducible but emotionally unrepeatable.
