# Leica Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 ASPH — Lens Profile

**Full name:** Leica Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 ASPH  
**Mount:** Leica M  
**Focal length:** 50mm (standard / "normal")  
**Maximum aperture:** f/1.4  
**Minimum aperture:** f/16  
**Minimum focus distance:** 0.7m  
**Construction:** 8 elements in 5 groups, 1 aspherical element  
**Era:** 2004–present (current ASPH version)  
**Previous versions:** Pre-ASPH (1959–1994), 1st ASPH (1994–2004)  

---

## Visual Identity

The Summilux-M 50mm f/1.4 ASPH is the lens that most people mean when they talk about "the Leica look." It is the standard by which all other standard lenses are judged — and it holds that position because of what it does at wide apertures: it renders subjects with a painterly, three-dimensional quality that is technically describable but emotionally difficult to explain.

At f/1.4 and f/2, the Summilux draws subjects out of their backgrounds with a combination of precise in-focus sharpness and a particular way of transitioning from sharp to unsharp that no other 50mm lens quite replicates. It is not as soft as the Noctilux — subjects are sharply rendered. But the out-of-focus areas are handled with a smoothness that prevents the background from competing with the subject for attention.

By f/4, the Summilux becomes one of the sharpest 50mm lenses ever made. Micro-contrast is exceptional. Edge rendition is crisp without appearing processed. At f/5.6–f/8, it is essentially optically perfect.

---

## Aperture by Aperture

### f/1.4 — Wide Open
**Character:** Painterly. Subject sharpness is precise at the focus point, but with a slight "drawing" quality — fine detail has a weight and softness that is distinct from digital sharpening. Focus falloff begins very close to the focus plane. The rendering is three-dimensional and luminous.

**Vignetting:** Noticeable — approximately 2 stops at the extreme corners. This is not a flaw; it is part of the look. The frame edges darken, focusing attention on the centre.

**Chromatic aberration:** Present at f/1.4, particularly in high-contrast edges against bright backgrounds. Visible as a slight fringing — warm/cool at opposing sides of sharp edges. This "character" is part of the Summilux wide-open rendering.

**Bokeh:** Smooth. Background highlights render as soft, slightly swirling circles with gentle, almost absent bright edges. Foreground bokeh has more structure — slightly nervous. This is typical of fast lenses.

**Use cases:** Portraits in available light, environmental portraits, documentary work. Any situation where subject isolation in available light is the priority.

---

### f/2 — Sweet Spot Begins
**Character:** The chromatic aberration largely disappears. Sharpness improves to outstanding. The three-dimensional rendering remains, but with more clarity. The transition zone between sharp and unsharp is slightly longer — more gradual depth of field.

**Vignetting:** Reduced to approximately 1 stop in corners.

**Best for:** Low-light documentary, street, indoor work.

---

### f/2.8–f/4 — The "Perfect" Zone
**Character:** Near-perfect optical performance. Sharpness is extremely high across the full frame. Micro-contrast is exceptional. Colours are rendered with full saturation and accuracy. No chromatic aberration.

**Vignetting:** Nearly gone.

**Best for:** Architecture, street in bright light, landscapes, group portraits.

---

### f/5.6–f/8 — Optimal Performance
Maximum sharpness. Diffraction not yet a factor. Corner sharpness equals centre performance. This is the Summilux at its technical peak — but the rendering is clean and objective rather than expressive.

---

## Optical Character

**Focus drawing:** The Summilux 50 ASPH has what photographers call a "3D pop" — subjects at the focus point appear to stand forward from the image plane, with backgrounds receding behind them. This is an interaction of micro-contrast, spherical aberration control, and focus falloff that is particularly strong with this lens.

**Colour transmission:** The Summilux's multi-coating produces a distinctive colour signature — slightly warm in the midtones, with very pure whites and deep blacks. Colours are rendered with a richness that is more characteristic of fine optics than of typical modern zoom lenses.

**Bokeh quality:** The signature strength of the Summilux. Background blur is smooth and calm. The out-of-focus areas do not distract — they recede. Bokeh circles are slightly soft-edged, with no harsh "nut ring" effect. In natural light with the right background, the Summilux produces a creamy background dissolution that is immediately recognisable.

**Flare resistance:** Good by Leica's current standards. The ASPH version handles backlight and direct sun with controlled flare — small, well-defined flare artefacts rather than large veiling glare. At f/8 pointed toward the sun, a characteristic starburst effect appears.

---

## In Different Conditions

**Portraits — natural light:** This is where the Summilux 50 excels. At f/1.4–f/2, in soft window light or open shade, it renders skin with a warmth and smoothness that is genuinely unlike any other lens. The three-dimensional quality is most visible in faces.

**Street — daytime:** At f/5.6–f/8 with zone focus, the Summilux is a precision documentary tool. High contrast, sharp, fast.

**Night / available light:** f/1.4 in available tungsten, neon, or mixed light. The slight colour fringing at f/1.4 becomes part of the character — warm light sources develop a slight halo, rendering night street scenes with a cinematic quality.

**Architecture:** At f/5.6–f/8, excellent sharpness and rendering of fine architectural detail. Slight barrel distortion is present (typical of 50mm lenses) — minimal and correctable.

## Combination Notes

**On M9 (CCD):** The CCD sensor's colour character combined with the Summilux's warmth produces the quintessential "Leica look" — warm, three-dimensional, analogue-feeling.

**On M10/M11 (CMOS):** Cleaner, more modern, but the lens character still comes through clearly. The 60MP M11 reveals every quality of the lens — and every quality of the photographer's technique.

**With Kodak Portra 400:** The warmth of the Summilux and Portra's skin tones are extremely complementary. Portrait work with this combination is benchmark quality.

**With Kodak Tri-X:** Wide open in available light, the Summilux's slight chromatic aberration becomes abstracted into fine grain, producing street images of great intensity.
